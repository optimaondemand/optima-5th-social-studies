🔍 Patriot or Loyalist: Two Sides, Real Reasons

Read both sides. A perspective is how something looks to one person or group.

🔍
Source Study: Two Sides

Read both sides carefully. Use these three questions on each one: What do I see? What does it mean? Why does it matter?

🦅 The Patriot Perspective

Core belief: The colonies had a right to govern themselves. Parliament, Britain’s law-making body, taxed them with no vote of theirs, and the King refused their petitions — written requests signed by many. The Declaration set out their case.

What they risked: Their land, families and lives. Signing was treason — helping your country’s enemies — against the Crown: the king and his government.

👑 The Loyalist Perspective

Core belief: The colonies belonged to the British Empire and owed loyalty to the Crown. Reform — changing the rules without breaking away — was the right path.

What they risked: Being treated badly, having their land taken, or leaving home for good. About 60,000 to 80,000 left during or after the war.

Who they were: Royal officials and rich merchants, but also ordinary farmers, shopkeepers, craftworkers, some ministers and recent arrivals. Some enslaved people joined the British, who promised freedom to those who escaped and fought for them.

⚖️
The Honest Editor's Hard Job

Both sides believed they were right. Both sides took real risks. An honest editor doesn’t pick a side first and then go looking for evidence. An honest editor reads the evidence first, then reports what it shows, even when the answer is complicated.

🔎 Question Like a Historian

What do I see?
What specific arguments did each side make? What evidence did they use?
What does it mean?
Why did careful people disagree about independence? Which good things were pulling against each other?
Why does it matter?
Why is it important to understand both perspectives — even the "losing" side? What does it teach us about honest reporting?
🦉🗞️
Historians estimate that about 40 to 45 in every 100 colonists were Patriots, 15 to 20 were Loyalists, and 35 to 40 stayed neutral — they took neither side. The Herald makes it sound like everyone agreed. An honest editor reports the full picture.
💡
Key Concept

The choice between Patriot and Loyalist was not obvious or simple. Both sides had real arguments, real fears, and real consequences. An honest editor presents both perspectives with evidence — not just the winning side's story.

🔎 Read the Sources: Two Title Pages, 1770

On 5 March 1770, British soldiers fired on a crowd in Boston and five people died. Within months, two packets about that night were printed. A title page is the front page of a book, with its name and its printer on it. Read both, and watch the words they choose.

🔤 The Word Desk: leg means “law”

Many English words are built on the Latin word lex, legis, “law.” You can spot it as leg.

  • legal (leg + al): allowed by the law
  • legislature (leg + islature): the group that makes laws
  • legitimate (leg + itimate): done by the rules

Discovery: The word loyal travelled into English from an old French word built on the Latin for lawful. A Loyalist thought keeping the king’s law was the loyal thing to do.

Try it: use the word legal in a sentence about today’s lesson.

★ Fact-Check Room ★
Before the Gazette Goes to Print
Check each claim carefully. An honest paper publishes only what holds up.
1 Which of the following best describes the debate between Patriots and Loyalists?
A Everyone living in the colonies agreed that breaking away from Britain was the only right choice to make.
B Colonists were deeply divided: many Patriots, many Loyalists, and many who stayed out of it.
C Only a few people cared about the debate, and most colonists ignored it entirely.
2 What was one key argument Loyalists made against independence?
A They believed the colonies should leave Britain and become part of France instead.
B They wanted to start a new separate country that was different from both Britain and the thirteen colonies.
C They feared a war would bring violence and ruin, and wanted change inside the British system.
3 What would an honest editor include when reporting on the Patriot-Loyalist debate?
A Both sides, with evidence: what each side believed, risked and argued for.
B Only what the Patriots said, because their side won the war in the end.
C Only what the Loyalists said, because their side is the one people hear about least.
