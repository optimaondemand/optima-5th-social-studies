📚 Understanding Colonial Diversity

The thirteen colonies were not one place with one story. They made up three regions (big areas whose places share land, weather and ways of earning a living). New England (Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island, New Hampshire) had rocky soil and cold winters, so people turned to fishing, shipbuilding and trade by sea. Towns ran their own town meetings, and church was at the center of town life. Most families had small farms. New England was more equal than the South, but not equal for everyone (5.07.02).

The Middle Colonies (New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware) had rich soil, milder winters and wide rivers. They grew so much wheat that they were called the breadbasket colonies. Philadelphia grew into the biggest city in the colonies. People of many backgrounds and faiths lived there, and William Penn’s Pennsylvania allowed religious tolerance (letting people worship in their own way) (5.07.03). The Southern Colonies (Maryland, Virginia, North and South Carolina, Georgia) had a warm climate and a long growing season. Plantations grew tobacco, rice and indigo to sell, mostly with the forced work of enslaved Africans. Rich planters led, though most free Southern families had small farms (5.07.04). Slavery was legal in all thirteen colonies by 1750, but the South depended on it most (5.07.07).

New England
Rocky Soil, Trade by Sea
Small farms, fishing and shipbuilding, town meetings, a strong church in every town.
Key features Cold winters, short growing season · Town meetings · Schools and Harvard College · Roger Williams founded Rhode Island · Slavery legal, with fewer enslaved people than the South (5.07.07)
Middle Colonies
Rich Land, Many Peoples
Wheat farms and flour mills, people from many places (Dutch, English, German, Scots-Irish, African and more), religious tolerance, busy port cities.
Key features Milder climate, rich soil · Philadelphia, the biggest city · William Penn founded Pennsylvania · Free and enslaved workers
Southern Colonies
Warm Land, Cash Crops
Plantations growing tobacco, rice and indigo to sell (cash crops), worked mostly by enslaved Africans; many small family farms too.
Key features Warm climate, long growing season · Rich planters led, as in Virginia’s House of Burgesses · James Oglethorpe founded Georgia · The South depended on slavery most

The Hasty Herald squeezed thirteen colonies into one story. But New England’s trade by sea, the Middle Colonies’ wheat and many peoples, and the South’s plantations made three very different places. They still traded with one another, and slavery was legal in all of them. A courteous editor gives each region its own story.

🔤 The Word Desk: geo means “earth”

Many English words come from the Greek word for earth. You can spot it as geo.

  • geography (geo + graphy): writing about the earth: its land, water and weather
  • geology (geo + logy): the study of rocks and the earth
  • geometry (geo + metry): measuring shapes (first used to measure land)

Discovery: The earth itself (soil, rivers, weather) made the three regions different.
