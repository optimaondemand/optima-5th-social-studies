🗞️ A Nation Divided: What Really Caused the Civil War?

The Hasty Herald says the Civil War was "just a disagreement between two regions." But the evidence tells a much deeper story. Today at the Front Page Desk, you'll investigate what actually tore the nation apart — and what it took to put it back together.

What did the war settle — and what questions did it leave unanswered?
⚡ Causes of the Civil War

Slavery was the central cause. Southern states depended on enslaved labor for their cotton economy. Northern states had largely abolished slavery and opposed its expansion into new territories.

Sectionalism — loyalty to one's region over the nation — deepened the divide. The North industrialized while the South remained agricultural.

States' rights — Southern leaders argued that states had the right to make their own laws, including laws about slavery. When Abraham Lincoln was elected in 1860, Southern states seceded, forming the Confederate States of America.

⚔️ The War and Its Toll

The Civil War lasted from 1861 to 1865 and was the deadliest conflict in American history — over 600,000 soldiers died.

Key events: Fort Sumter (first shots), the Emancipation Proclamation (Lincoln freed enslaved people in Confederate states, 1863), the Battle of Gettysburg (turning point), and Lee's surrender at Appomattox (1865).

Frederick Douglass, a formerly enslaved man, was a powerful voice for abolition and equality throughout the war.

🏛️ Reconstruction and the Amendments

After the war, Reconstruction (1865–1877) was the effort to rebuild the South and integrate formerly enslaved people into American society. The Freedmen's Bureau helped with food, housing, schools, and employment.

Three Constitutional amendments transformed the nation:

• The 13th Amendment (1865) abolished slavery throughout the United States.

• The 14th Amendment (1868) granted citizenship to all persons born in the U.S. and guaranteed equal protection under the law.

• The 15th Amendment (1870) protected the right to vote regardless of race.

These amendments were enormous steps — but in practice, Southern states found ways to limit Black citizens' rights for nearly another century through Black Codes, Jim Crow laws, and voter suppression.

🦉📰
The Herald says the Civil War was "just a disagreement." But a disagreement is about where to eat dinner. The Civil War was about whether millions of human beings could be held as property. That's not a disagreement — that's a moral crisis. Good editors use precise language.
💡
Key Concept

The Civil War was caused by slavery, sectionalism, and the states' rights debate — not just a regional disagreement. The 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments transformed the Constitution, but the struggle for true equality continued long after Reconstruction ended.
