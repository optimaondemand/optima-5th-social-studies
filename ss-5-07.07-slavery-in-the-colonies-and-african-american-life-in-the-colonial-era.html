📚 Slavery Denied Dignity; Enslaved People Resisted

Slavery in colonial America was not just a way of getting work done. It was built to take away people’s freedom and dignity, the respect every person deserves. A 1662 Virginia law said a child born to an enslaved mother was enslaved for life, so slavery passed from parent to child. Laws in 1705 called enslaved people property, to be bought and sold. After 1740, South Carolina made it a crime for enslaved people to gather in groups or use drums, and for anyone to teach them to write (Georgia copied much of this law in 1755). No colony’s law protected an enslaved family, so families could be sold apart. Whipping and violence were used to force obedience. By 1750, slavery was legal in all thirteen colonies, North and South.

Yet enslaved Africans and African Americans resisted (pushed back) again and again. Some worked slowly, broke tools or pretended to be sick. Many ran away, some all the way to Spanish Florida. In 1739, about 20 enslaved men started the Stono Rebellion (an uprising) in South Carolina and marched south toward Florida, calling out “Liberty!” People in New York City rose up in 1712. Through all of it, people kept their families together when they could, and kept African songs, drumming, stories, prayers and words alive. On the South Carolina and Georgia coast they shaped the Gullah language. Some Black colonists were free, like Anthony Johnson, who owned a farm in Virginia in the 1650s, though a free Black person’s freedom was never fully safe. An honest account of slavery has to include the humanity, the choices and the resistance of enslaved people themselves.

⚠️ System of Oppression
How Slavery Denied Dignity
Slavery was built to take freedom, rights and dignity away from human beings, using laws, violence and control over every part of life.
Evidence Slavery passed from parent to child (Virginia, 1662) · Treated as property (1705) · Banned from gathering in groups, using drums or learning to write (South Carolina, 1740) · Families sold apart at an auction (a public sale) · Violence and whipping
🌟 Acts of Resistance
How Enslaved People Resisted
Enslaved Africans and African Americans pushed back against cruel, unfair control and kept their humanity: they rose up, escaped, and kept their culture alive.
Evidence Running away · Rebellions (Stono, 1739; New York City, 1712) · Working slowly · Keeping families connected · African songs, drumming and stories · The Gullah language

The Hasty Herald called slavery just labor. But slavery took away the dignity of millions of people. And enslaved people were not people who just gave up: they resisted in many ways and kept their humanity against terrible odds. An honest editor reports both the cruelty of slavery and the courage of the people who lived through it and resisted it.

🌴 African Americans in Early Florida

Some enslaved people found a road to freedom to the south. In 1693, the king of Spain promised freedom to people who escaped from the English colonies and reached Spanish Florida, if they became Catholic (joined the Catholic Church) and helped defend the colony. In 1738 they built Fort Mose, about two miles north of St. Augustine. It was the first legally free Black settlement in what is now the United States. Its captain, Francisco Menéndez, had escaped slavery in Carolina himself.

Later, many people who escaped slavery joined the Seminole people, a Native American nation in Florida. They became known as Black Seminoles, living, farming, and fighting alongside the Seminoles.

🔤 The Word Desk: dign means “worthy”

Many English words come from the Latin word dignus, which means worthy (deserving respect). You can spot it as dign.

  • dignity (dign + ity): being worthy of respect, just for being human
  • dignified (dign + ified): calm and worthy of respect
  • indignant (in + dign + ant): angry about something unfair
  • indignity (in + dign + ity): treatment that takes away a person’s dignity

Discovery: Slavery laws treated people as property, an indignity. But no law could make a person less worthy. That is why an honest editor writes about dignity, not just work.
