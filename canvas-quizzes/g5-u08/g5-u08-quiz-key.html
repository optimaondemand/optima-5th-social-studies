1. Britain won the French and Indian War in 1763. What happened next?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.08.01 · The Desk (what the war cost Britain)

  1. Britain gave the colonies seats in Parliament and a vote on taxes.
  2. France took control of the thirteen colonies and ruled them from Paris.
  3. Britain had a large war debt, so Parliament taxed the colonies. ✓
  4. Britain stopped collecting taxes in the colonies for ten years.

Right answer: C

Feedback when right: Right. Winning the war left Britain deep in debt, so Parliament looked to the colonies to help pay it. Britain also drew the Proclamation Line of 1763 and told colonists not to settle west of it.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.01. Britain won the war but came out of it with a large debt, and new taxes followed.

2. Colonists said “No taxation without representation.” What did they mean?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.08.02 · The Desk (the acts and the grievances)

  1. They had no elected members in Parliament. ✓
  2. They thought all taxes were wrong, anywhere in the world.
  3. They wanted France to collect the taxes instead of Britain.
  4. They thought only rich colonists should pay any taxes.

Right answer: A

Feedback when right: Right. Parliament taxed the colonists, but no colonist had been elected to sit in it and argue for them.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.02. The argument was about who gets a say, not about whether taxes should exist.

3. Some colonists took neither side in the argument about independence. What were they called?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.08.03 · The Desk (three groups of colonists)

  1. Patriots
  2. Loyalists
  3. Neutral ✓
  4. Ministers

Right answer: C

Feedback when right: Right. Neutral means taking neither side, and it was a large group — roughly a third of colonists.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.03. Patriots wanted independence and Loyalists wanted to stay British. Neutral colonists chose neither.

4. The Declaration of Independence says people have unalienable rights. What does unalienable mean?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.08.04 · The Desk (unalienable rights)

  1. Given only to leaders
  2. Able to be bought
  3. Written down in law
  4. Cannot be taken away ✓

Right answer: D

Feedback when right: Right. The Declaration argues people are born with these rights, so no government can take them away.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.04. Unalienable rights are rights nobody can take from you.

5. The Declaration said “all men are created equal.” Which statement about 1776 is accurate?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.08.04 · The Desk (what the words did and did not do) · reasoning

  1. Those words gave the same rights to everyone living in the colonies.
  2. Enslaved people, women and Native nations did not get those rights. ✓
  3. The words were added to the Declaration a hundred years later.
  4. Only the men who signed the Declaration were covered by the words.

Right answer: B

Feedback when right: Right. The words were powerful, and they did not reach everyone in 1776. People used them later to argue for rights they had been denied.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.04. The idea was bigger than what the law actually did in 1776.

6. Which of these helped the American Revolution? Choose every right answer.

Multiple answers · 1 point · From: 5.08.05 · The Desk (the coalition)

  1. Haym Salomon, who raised money to pay for the war ✓
  2. Wentworth Cheswell, who rode to warn colonists and served as a soldier ✓
  3. The First Rhode Island Regiment, which included Black and Native soldiers ✓
  4. Captain Thomas Preston, who commanded the British soldiers in Boston

Right answer: A, B, C

Feedback when right: Right. The Revolution ran on a wide group of people: someone had to pay for it, carry warnings and fight.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.05. Salomon, Cheswell and the First Rhode Island Regiment all worked for the Revolution. Captain Preston commanded British soldiers.

7. Historians have written records showing Deborah Sampson served as a soldier. For “Molly Pitcher” they have stories but no such records. What should a careful historian say?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.08.06 · The Desk (record or legend) · reasoning

  1. Sampson’s service is documented; Molly Pitcher is partly legend. ✓
  2. Both stories rest on exactly the same kind of evidence.
  3. Neither woman did anything during the Revolution at all.
  4. A good story is stronger evidence than a written record.

Right answer: A

Feedback when right: Right. Documented means there are records. Saying so honestly is not the same as saying the legend is worthless.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.06. One of these has records behind it and one does not, and a careful historian says which is which.

8. Match each change to what it did.

Matching (1 point, split across 3 matches) · 1 point · From: 5.08.07 · The Desk (the year slider)

  • The 15th Amendment (1870) → A man could not be kept from voting because of his race
  • The 19th Amendment (1920) → Women could not be kept from voting because they are women
  • The 26th Amendment (1971) → The voting age dropped from 21 to 18

Extra choice (matches nothing): Everyone in the country could vote for the first time

Right answer: See the matches above

Feedback when right: Right. The vote widened one step at a time, and people had to push for every step.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.07 and the year slider: 1870 was about race, 1920 about women, 1971 about age.

9. Two packets about the night of 5 March 1770 were both printed in 1770. One calls it an “unhappy disturbance.” The other calls it a “horrid massacre.” What does that show?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.08.08 · The Desk (the two 1770 title pages) · reasoning

  1. One packet was printed many years after the other one.
  2. Only one of the two packets was really printed in 1770.
  3. The two packets agree about everything that happened.
  4. Two sources about one event can describe it very differently. ✓

Right answer: D

Feedback when right: Right. Both were printed close to the event, and they still disagree. Being close to an event does not make a source complete.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.08. Both title pages are dated 1770 — the difference is in the words each side chose.

10. John Adams was a Patriot. In 1770 he defended the British soldiers in court. Why?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.08.08 · The Desk (Did you know? A Patriot defended the soldiers)

  1. He had changed sides and become a Loyalist.
  2. He believed every person deserves a fair trial. ✓
  3. The king had ordered him to take the case.
  4. He wanted the soldiers to be found guilty.

Right answer: B

Feedback when right: Right. He stayed a Patriot. He took the case because he thought a fair trial mattered more than which side you were on.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.08.08. Adams did not change sides, and he was not ordered to do it.
