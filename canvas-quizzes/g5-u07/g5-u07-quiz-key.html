1. Most colonists came for money, faith or a fresh start. Which people were brought to the colonies by force?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.07.01 · The Desk (who came, and why)

  1. Indentured servants
  2. Quakers
  3. Enslaved Africans ✓
  4. Puritans

Right answer: C

Feedback when right: Right. Enslaved Africans did not choose to come; they were captured and forced across the ocean.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.07.01. Indentured servants agreed to work for years to pay for the trip; enslaved Africans had no choice.

2. What did William Penn’s 1701 charter for Pennsylvania allow?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.07.03 · The Desk (Penn’s Pennsylvania)

  1. People who believed in God could worship freely ✓
  2. Only Quakers could live in Pennsylvania
  3. Anyone could hold a government job
  4. Slavery was against the law

Right answer: A

Feedback when right: Right. Pennsylvania allowed people who believed in God to worship freely, though only Christians could hold government jobs.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.07.03. Penn’s charter allowed freedom of worship for believers in God; slavery was still legal there.

3. Why did many New Englanders fish, build ships and trade instead of running big farms?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.07.02 · The Desk (geography and work)

  1. The king ordered them to
  2. New England had no forests or harbors
  3. The weather was warm all year
  4. Rocky soil and short summers made big farms hard ✓

Right answer: D

Feedback when right: Right. Geography shaped New England: rocky soil and short summers pushed people toward the sea.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.07.02. New England had rocky soil, short summers, forests and good harbors.

4. Match each region to the way most people there earned a living.

Matching (1 point, split across 3 matches) · 1 point · From: 5.07.02–5.07.04, 5.07.08 · The Desk (Ask the three regions)

  • New England → Fishing, shipbuilding and trade by sea
  • Middle Colonies → Wheat farms and flour mills
  • Southern Colonies → Tobacco, rice and indigo plantations

Extra choice (matches nothing): Gold and silver mines

Right answer: See the matches above

Feedback when right: Right. Each region earned its living from its own land and water.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at the chart in 5.07.08, or at 5.07.02, 5.07.03 and 5.07.04.

5. Most free families in the Southern Colonies were…

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.07.04 · The Desk (who lived in the South)

  1. rich planters with large plantations
  2. merchants who lived in London
  3. small farmers ✓
  4. overseers on rice plantations

Right answer: C

Feedback when right: Right. Rich planters had the most power, but most free Southern families were small farmers.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.07.04. Planters were a small, powerful group; most free families had small farms.

6. What shaped what a colonist’s day looked like? Choose ALL that are right.

Multiple answers · 1 point · From: 5.07.05 · The Desk (whose daily life?)

  1. Where they lived ✓
  2. How much money they had ✓
  3. Whether they were free or enslaved ✓
  4. Nothing: daily life was the same in every colony

Right answer: A, B, C

Feedback when right: Right. Region, wealth, race and whether a person was free or enslaved all shaped daily life.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.07.05. Daily life was very different from person to person and place to place.

7. In the triangular trade, which leg carried enslaved Africans across the Atlantic?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.07.06 · The Desk (the three legs of the trade)

  1. Africa to the Americas, called the Middle Passage ✓
  2. Europe to Africa, carrying cloth and guns
  3. The Americas to Europe, carrying sugar and tobacco
  4. Boston to Philadelphia, carrying fish

Right answer: A

Feedback when right: Right. The Middle Passage carried millions of captive Africans to the Americas, and many died on the way.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.07.06. Leg 2, the Middle Passage, went from Africa to the Americas.

8. What was Fort Mose, built in 1738?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.07.07 · The Desk (Fort Mose, card 5)

  1. A British fort that guarded Georgia
  2. A rice plantation in South Carolina
  3. A town in Virginia where the 1662 law was written
  4. A free Black town in Spanish Florida, built by people who escaped slavery ✓

Right answer: D

Feedback when right: Right. Fort Mose, near St. Augustine, was the first legally free Black town in what is now the United States.

Feedback when wrong: Look back at 5.07.07, card 5: people who escaped slavery in Carolina built Fort Mose in Spanish Florida.

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.07.08 · The Desk (Mark up the 1755 map: shown / can’t tell)

  2. Which colonies were on the coast?
  3. Who did most of the work on Southern plantations? ✓

10. An editor writes: “One region cannot stand for all thirteen colonies.” Which detail BEST supports that claim?

Multiple choice · 1 point · From: 5.07.08 · Go to Press (evidence for a correction)

  1. New England fished and traded, while the South grew tobacco and rice on plantations ✓
  2. By 1750, slavery was legal in all thirteen colonies
  3. The Old Ship Meetinghouse was built in 1681
  4. Ships carried flour from New York to Boston

Right answer: A

Feedback when right: Right. This detail shows two regions earning a living in very different ways.

Feedback when wrong: That detail is true, but it shows something the regions shared or a single place. Look back at rows 1–4 of the chart in 5.07.08.
