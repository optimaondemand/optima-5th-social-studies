📚 Rocky Soil, Strong Beliefs, and Town Meetings

The New England colonies were Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island and New Hampshire. Their soil was thin and rocky, and winters were long and cold, so the growing season was short. Big plantations could not work there. Families ran small farms to feed themselves, and many people made a living from the forest and the sea: cutting lumber, fishing for cod, building ships and trading. Boston became a busy trading port.

Beliefs shaped New England as much as the land did. In 1630, Puritans started the Massachusetts Bay Colony. Puritans were English Protestants who wanted a stricter, plainer church. Their leader, John Winthrop, said they would be “as a city upon a hill,” a model community everyone would watch. Puritans believed every person should read the Bible, so they started schools: a school in Boston in 1635, Harvard College in 1636, and a 1647 law that made every town with 50 families hire a teacher. Each town built a meetinghouse, used for church and for town meetings, where the men of the town voted on local rules.

Not everyone had a say. Only men voted at town meetings, and for many years only church members could vote for the colony’s leaders. Puritans wanted freedom to worship their own way, not freedom for everyone: in 1635 Roger Williams was expelled (forced to leave) for saying the government should not make rules about religion. In 1636 he started Providence, in Rhode Island, where people could worship in different ways. Native nations, including the Wampanoag, Narragansett and Pequot, lost land and lives in wars with the colonists. Slavery was legal in New England too.

🏔️ Geography’s Role
What the Land Shaped
Rocky soil, cold winters, forests and harbors shaped what New Englanders could grow, build and trade.
Evidence Rocky soil → small farms, not plantations · Harbors → fishing, shipbuilding, trade · Forests → lumber · Cold winters → a short growing season
⛪ Religion’s Role
What Beliefs Shaped
Puritan beliefs made reading, schools and town meetings a big part of New England life.
Evidence Town meetings to make local rules · Schools so children could read the Bible (1647 school law) · Harvard College (1636) · Roger Williams expelled → Rhode Island, where people could worship in different ways

The Hasty Herald said colonists “simply preferred it.” Some of it was a choice: the Puritans chose to come for their beliefs. But rocky soil pushed people toward the sea, Puritan beliefs led to schools and town meetings, and many people, like women, enslaved people, Native nations and people who disagreed, got no say at all. The land, beliefs and work shaped New England together.
