📚 What Was the French and Indian War?

In the mid-1700s, Britain and France both claimed the Ohio Valley, rich land west of the Appalachian Mountains. Native nations, such as the Shawnee and Lenape, already lived there, and no one asked them. These competing claims (saying land belongs to you) started a war that lasted from 1754 to 1763.

In 1754, Virginia sent 22-year-old George Washington and the colony’s militia (part-time soldiers) to drive the French out. The French made him surrender at Fort Necessity. Britain declared war in 1756. Colonists fought on Britain’s side, and Native nations chose sides for their own reasons. Britain won big victories in 1758 and 1759, at Fort Duquesne and at Quebec.

The Treaty of Paris (1763), the peace agreement, ended the war. France gave Britain nearly all its land east of the Mississippi River. Britain had won, but at a high cost.

Why the War Started

📜 Looking Ahead: Rights, Voice, and a Famous Document

During the war, colonists governed themselves through elected assemblies and town meetings, but they had no representatives (people elected to speak for them) in Parliament. (Today, citizens vote for members of Congress.) When Britain taxed the colonies to pay its debt, colonists said their rights were ignored. In 1776 they explained those rights in the Declaration of Independence, which says all people have unalienable rights, rights no government can take away. It is still one of the most important documents and symbols of the United States.

★ Key Concept

The French and Indian War did not simply cause the Revolution. It set the stage: Britain, short of money, began taxing colonists who believed they had earned their rights. The Herald’s headline skips the middle, and the middle is the story.

🦉
Opti's Fun Fact

George Washington was only 22 when he first led soldiers, in 1754. His defeat at Fort Necessity taught him lessons about leadership he used in the Revolutionary War.

Here’s what the Hasty Herald missed: after the war, Britain needed someone to pay the bills. Parliament decided that the colonists, who had been protected by Britain’s army, should share the cost. New taxes and trade laws followed. The Proclamation of 1763 also said colonists could not settle west of the Appalachian Mountains, the very land they had helped win. Colonists felt taxed and blocked, with no say in Parliament. The war didn’t just end. It planted the anger that grew into the Revolution.

🔤 The Word Desk: claim / clam means “call out”

Many English words come from the Latin word clamare, to call out or shout. You can spot it as claim or clam.

  • claim: to call out that something is yours or is true
  • proclamation (pro + clam + ation): a public calling out, an official announcement
  • exclaim (ex + claim): to cry out

Discovery: Britain and France both claimed the Ohio Valley. In 1763 the king’s proclamation called out a new rule for the land.

Before the Gazette Goes to Print
Check each claim carefully. An honest paper publishes only what holds up.
1 What was the main cause of the French and Indian War?
A Britain and France both claimed the Ohio Valley and were determined to control it.
B Britain wanted to end the fur trade in North America.
C The colonists rebelled against British taxes, which started the fighting.
2 How did the French and Indian War lead to conflict between Britain and its colonies?
A Britain rewarded the colonies with lower taxes and more self-government after the war.
B The colonies refused to help fight the war, so Britain punished them by canceling all trade.
C Britain's massive war debt led Parliament to tax the colonies and restrict westward settlement, angering colonists.
3 The Hasty Herald printed: "The French and Indian War ended, and then the Revolution simply began." What should an honest editor do with this headline?
A Print it — the war did end before the Revolution, so the headline is accurate enough.
B Correct it — the headline skips the key middle steps: Britain's debt, new taxes, the Proclamation of 1763, and growing colonial anger.
C Ignore it — the Herald's job is to be fast, not accurate, so their claim doesn't matter.
