🗞️ The Divided Colonies: Who Stood Where?

It’s easy to picture every colonist marching toward independence. The real story is more complicated. Colonists were deeply divided, and each group had real reasons for its choice.

🗞️
Three Groups, Three Perspectives

Read for each group’s reasons, not just its label. The numbers are historians’ estimates (careful guesses). No one took a count back then.

Patriots

About 40–45% of colonists. By 1776, they wanted independence from Britain. They believed in natural rights (rights every person is born with) and self-governance (people making their own laws). The Declaration of Independence later called these unalienable rights. Leaders included Samuel Adams, Patrick Henry and the Sons of Liberty (a Patriot protest group).

Loyalists

About 15–20% of colonists. They stayed loyal to Britain and the Crown (the king and his government). They feared war and disorder. They included merchants who traded with Britain, royal officials, and some enslaved people who joined the British after a 1775 offer of freedom.

Neutral / Undecided

About 35–40% of colonists, one of the largest groups. Many were farmers and families who wanted peace. Some, like Quakers, would not fight because of their faith. Many changed sides as armies came and went. Staying neutral (on neither side) was risky too: both sides pushed them to pick a side.

🔍 Two Voices from the Colonies

Notice what each side values and fears. Both speeches were written for this lesson to sum up each side. They are not real quotes.

Patriot side · example written for this lesson

We have the natural right to govern ourselves. Parliament taxes us without our consent, and not one colonist voted for it. If we do not act now, our children will lose their liberty.

Loyalist side · example written for this lesson

Britain has protected us for over a century. Our trade, our safety and our laws rest on the Crown. Breaking away means war and the loss of everything we have built.

⚖️
Cause → Choice → Consequence

Cause: Parliament passed acts that colonists said broke their rights, and the colonies had no vote in those decisions.

Choice: Every colonist had to decide: resist British rule, stay loyal to the Crown, or try to stay out of it.

Consequence: Families were divided. Neighbors became enemies. The road to revolution was not a parade. It was a crack that split towns apart.

🗳️ Choosing, Then and Now

Women chose too. Many Patriot women signed pledges to boycott (refuse to buy) British goods, and many Loyalist women lost their homes when their families fled. In 1774, towns pushed people to sign pledges. Today, citizens choose by voting in private, and no one can force you to sign.

🦉🗞️
Here’s something many people don’t realize: about a third of colonists tried not to pick a side. Staying out took its own kind of courage when neighbors were pushing.
💡
Key Concept

The American Revolution was not unanimous (something every person agreed on). Colonists were divided into Patriots, Loyalists and many who were neutral, and each group had real reasons for its choices.

🔤 The Word Desk: patr means “father”

Many English words come from the Latin and Greek words for father (pater). You can spot it as patr or pater.

  • patriot (patr + iot): a person who loves and defends their fatherland, or country
  • paternal (pater + nal): like a father, or from the father’s side
  • patron (patr + on): a person who supports someone, the way a father might

Discovery: Patriot comes from the Greek word patris, “fatherland.” Patriots said they were defending their homeland.

Try it: use the word patriot in a sentence about today’s lesson.

★ Fact-Check Room ★
Before the Gazette Goes to Print
Check each claim carefully. An honest paper publishes only what holds up.
1 About how many colonists actively supported independence during the Revolution?
A About 40–45% — a large group, but not a majority of all colonists.
B Nearly 100% — everyone wanted freedom from Britain.
C Fewer than 5% — almost no one supported the Revolution.
2 What does the evidence show about colonists who remained neutral?
A They were cowards who refused to take any stand.
B They were secretly working for the British military.
C They were a large group, and many feared the consequences of picking either side.
3 What would a courageous editor include when reporting on colonial opinions?
A Only the Patriot perspective, because they won the war.
B All three perspectives — Patriot, Loyalist, and neutral — with the reasons behind each.
C A claim that everyone agreed, because disagreement is too complicated to explain.
