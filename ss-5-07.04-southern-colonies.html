📚 Cash Crops, Plantations, and Enslaved Labor

The Southern Colonies were Virginia, Maryland, North Carolina, South Carolina and Georgia. They had warm weather, long growing seasons and rich soil along wide rivers. Those were good conditions for cash crops, crops grown to sell: tobacco in Virginia and Maryland, and rice and indigo (a plant used to make blue dye) in the Carolinas. Cotton came much later. Plantations, very large farms that sometimes covered thousands of acres, grew these crops to send to Britain and Europe.

But the plantations depended on the forced work of enslaved Africans and African Americans. By the 1700s, enslaved people were a large part of the Southern population. In South Carolina in 1750 they were more than half. They worked from sunrise to sunset for no pay, could be bought and sold, and faced harsh punishment if they resisted. Families were split up when people were sold. The planters’ wealth was built on this work. A small group of rich planters was on top, small farmers and tradespeople were in the middle, and enslaved people were at the bottom, with no legal rights. Slavery was legal in all thirteen colonies, but the South depended on it most.

The South was more than farms, too. Charles Town was a busy port with merchants and printers. Virginia’s House of Burgesses, first held in 1619, was the first elected group of lawmakers in the English colonies, but only white men who owned land could vote for it. An honest report of the Southern Colonies includes the crops, the government, AND the harm done to people.

💰 Who Got the Money
Wealthy Planters
A small group of rich plantation owners controlled the land, the money and the colonial government.
Evidence Tobacco, rice and indigo sent to Britain · Thousands of acres per plantation · Only white men who owned land could vote for the House of Burgesses · Most of the power
⛓️ Who Paid the Price
Enslaved Africans and African Americans
The plantations ran on the forced work of enslaved people, who had no freedom, no rights and no choice.
Evidence Forced work from sunrise to sunset · Bought and sold as property · Families split up by sales · Harsh punishment used to control them · No legal rights

The Hasty Herald called the Southern Colonies “just farms and plantations.” There were plantations, yes. But they were built on the work of enslaved people, and they made some people rich while others suffered. A persevering editor reports both the crops and the harm done to people, because the people who built the South deserve an honest account.

🔤 The Word Desk: port means “carry”

Many English words come from the Latin word portare, which means to carry. You can spot it as port.

  • export (ex + port): to carry goods out to sell in another place (ex means out)
  • import (im + port): to carry goods in from another place (im means in)
  • transport (trans + port): to carry across from one place to another (trans means across)
  • porter (port + er): a person whose job is carrying loads

Discovery: Southern planters exported tobacco, rice and indigo to Britain, and imported cloth, tools and books.

Try it: use the word export in a sentence about the Southern Colonies.
