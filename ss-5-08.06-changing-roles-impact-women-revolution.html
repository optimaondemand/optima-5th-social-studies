🔍 Women in the Revolution: More Than Spectators

The Hasty Herald says women were spectators, people who only watch. Today you’ll check the records. Some stories about women in the war are documented: written down at the time. Others are legends: stories that grew as people retold them.

Running Farms and Businesses

When men went to war, many women ran the family farm, shop or trade alone. They grew food, kept the accounts and made goods. Families and the army depended on this work.

Boycotts

A boycott means refusing to buy something to make a point. Groups like the Daughters of Liberty spun their own cloth instead of buying British imports, goods brought in from another country.

In the Army

Thousands of camp followers traveled with the Continental Army to cook, wash and nurse. Deborah Sampson disguised herself as a man and served 17 months as a soldier. The famous “Molly Pitcher” story is part legend: Mary Ludwig Hays, the woman most often linked to it, carried water and helped work a cannon at Monmouth in 1778. “Agent 355” appears in just one 1779 spy letter, and no one knows who she was.

Voices for Rights

Abigail Adams wrote to her husband John in 1776: “Remember the Ladies.” She asked the leaders writing new laws to be fair to women, and warned that women would not obey laws in which they had “no voice.”

The Courageous Editor's Question

After the Revolution, most women still could not vote or hold office. (One state, New Jersey, let some women who owned property vote until 1807.) A courageous editor reports both what women did and what they were denied.

🦉🗞️
Deborah Sampson served for 17 months before a doctor found out she was a woman. Records from the time prove it, so a careful paper can print it.
💡
Key Concept

Women played essential roles in the American Revolution, at home, in boycotts, with the army and in speaking up for rights, even though most were denied the vote afterward.

🔤 The Word Desk: spect means “look”

Many English words come from the Latin word spectare, “to watch.” You can spot it as spect.

  • spectator (spect + at + or): a person who watches but doesn’t take part
  • inspect (in + spect): to look at closely
  • respect (re + spect): to look up to someone

Discovery: The Herald called women spectators. The records show women who did much more than watch.

Try it: use the word spectator in a sentence about today’s lesson.

★ Fact-Check Room ★
Before the Gazette Goes to Print
Check each claim carefully. An honest paper publishes only what holds up.
1 Which of the following best describes women's roles during the American Revolution?
A Women had no involvement in the Revolution at all.
B Women only sewed flags and cooked meals for soldiers.
C Women ran farms and businesses, organized boycotts, served as spies and soldiers, and advocated for rights.
2 What did Abigail Adams mean when she wrote "Remember the ladies"?
A She was asking the leaders to make laws that are fair to women and give them a voice.
B She wanted women to be allowed to attend military parades.
C She wanted a holiday named after women in the Revolution.
3 What would a courageous editor include when reporting on women in the Revolution?
A Only the story of Betsy Ross, because she is the most famous woman from that era.
B The full range of women's contributions — economic, political, military, and intellectual — along with what women were still denied.
C A note that women's contributions were minor compared to men's.
